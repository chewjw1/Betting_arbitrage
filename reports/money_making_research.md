# Money-Making Avenues: Research Findings

Two markets researched in depth (live web data, 2025–2026), pressure-tested
against the constraint set: **solo technical operator, prefers to sell things
once (not subscriptions), no customer-service/relationship business, open to
"legal but icky," wants new/risky, can't tie up capital for years.**

Sourcing note: strongest anchors are Circana BookScan, Variety/Companies House
filings, ALLi & Written Word Media surveys, Amazon KDP pages, and named
journalism (Fortune, Reuters, KQED, Rolling Stone). Aggregator/SEO figures are
flagged where load-bearing. Reddit was not directly fetchable; community
sentiment came via journalism and aggregators.

---

## Bottom line

Both markets are real and large. **Neither cleanly fits the stated
constraints**, for the same structural reason every avenue in this whole
exploration has hit: *passive + easy + no-relationship + high-margin* gets
competed to zero, so what's left pays for either sustained creative output
(romance) or sustained SaaS/service (adult tools).

- **Romance ebooks** — real money at the top, but it's a *capital-and-stamina
  writing career on rented land* that *punishes* the AI-leverage plan. Viable
  only if you genuinely want to write a series for 1–2+ years.
- **Adult-creator tools** — real market, genuinely underserved gaps, but the
  gaps are *service/subscription/financial-entity shaped*, carry a 10–14%
  payment tax and ToS/rented-land risk, and the cleanest solo build (forensic
  watermarking + leak removal) is contested and technically hard.
- **Best fit for the constraints remains a one-time-product niche elsewhere**
  (STL/parametric designs, datasets, a sell-once tool) where build-skill is the
  moat.

## Romance / erotica ebooks

**Market.** Romance is the highest-earning fiction genre (~$1.44B/yr, ~2× the
next genre; ~58% of Amazon's most-sold ebooks; ebooks ~60% of romance units).
Indie authors capture ~two-thirds of Amazon romance ebook revenue, and **44% of
indie authors earning $10k+/month write romance** (vs 21% of all indies). Print
romance grew +24% YoY in 2025 (Circana) — romantasy and sports romance at
triple-digit growth.

**Income math (the sobering part).** KU pays ~$0.0042–$0.0048/page (Jan 2026
$0.004204; Apr 2026 ~$0.0048). Distribution is a power law:
- Median *committed* indie author ~$13,500/yr (ALLi 2025); full-population
  median ~$2,000 (Authors Guild); ~75–77% earn under $1,000/yr; 90% of self-pub
  books sell <100 copies.
- Income tracks **catalog size**: ~20 books → ~$20k/yr; **50+ books → $100k+/yr**;
  25+ books → median ~$3k/month. Debut/year-1 is typically $0–few-hundred and
  often net-negative.
- Email list is the strongest single income correlate (~20× gap with vs
  without).

**Per-book cost stack (recurring, ~monthly cadence).** Cover $100–880+
(illustrated romance >$1,000); Amazon Ads ~$300–450/launch (CPCs inflating,
~$1+ and rising); BookBub Featured Deal $500–3,000. Successful authors spend
$1,000–5,000/month on ads and 10–15 hrs/week marketing.

**Best sub-niche openings (rising demand, thin supply — least likely to be
crushed):**
1. **Sapphic/lesbian romance** — +110% growth 2025, far thinner supply (~3,689
   sapphic vs ~6,332 gay titles); strongest demand-vs-supply signal.
2. **Monster romance** (non-orc folklore: centaur, Greek/Japanese, etc.) —
   indie-native because trad publishers avoid it; modest title counts.
3. **Paranormal-rebound** — riding romantasy nostalgia; ~2.5× over-represented
   among top indie earners; less crowded than romantasy.
4. Non-hockey **sports romance**; micro-niches with <2,000 titles generally.

**Avoid:** contemporary and paranormal-broad (saturated), **erotica shorts**
(Amazon de-emphasizing short books + price compression + content bans).

**Risks.** (a) AI-slop floods discoverability — though the hard volume data is
mostly *non-fiction*; romance is a taste/loyalty market where genuine quality
plausibly differentiates *more*, and **visible AI use is reputational poison**
(BookTok torches authors caught with leaked prompts). (b) **Platform risk:** KDP
cold-terminates accounts (forfeiting unpaid royalties), and the "adult dungeon"
filter can make spicy titles unsearchable; content-policy lines (incest/noncon/
age) shift and are inconsistently enforced. (c) Burnout from the monthly-release
treadmill is a documented failure mode.

**Verdict:** Viable and lucrative *if you commit to writing a series in an
underserved sub-niche for 1–2+ years on real human quality.* Not passive, not
sell-once, and it punishes the AI shortcut.

## Adult-creator tools

**Market.** OnlyFans alone: $7.2B GMV (FY2024), $5.8B paid to 4.6M creator
accounts, >$25B paid since 2016 (now valued ~$3.15B after the founder's death).
Power law: top 10% of creators take ~73% of revenue (median ~$180/mo), so the
realistic *paying* customer base is the top ~10–25% — low-hundreds-of-thousands
of creators — plus a fast-growing AI-creator wedge (Fanvue ~$200M ARR).

**Saturation map:**
- **Crowded (avoid):** DMCA/leak-takedown (15+ vendors, price war $15–324/mo),
  management/CRM suites (Infloww $40–500/acct, OnlyMonster, Supercreator,
  CreatorHero), AI-chat/sexting (20+ tools), scheduling/mass-DM, link-in-bio,
  age-verification *providers* (12+ funded vendors, per-check prices collapsing
  to pennies), generalist tax tools.
- **Thin gaps:** creator-priced **forensic watermarking + leak removal**;
  dedicated **promo/S4S networks** (mostly informal); **adult-native
  bookkeeping**; **agency-vetting**; **secure delegated access** (no native
  role-based permissions → password-sharing); **chargeback protection** (the
  single clearest white space — no product exists, only a petition).

**Why those gaps stay open (the "legal but icky" moat):** Stripe/PayPal/Square
prohibit adult; the compliant route is high-risk processors at **~10.8–14.5%**
(vs ~2.9%) + rolling reserves. No official OnlyFans/Fansly API → tools
reverse-engineer private endpoints (ToS-violating, breaks often, gets creators
banned). Apple/Google reject adult apps → web/PWA only. Founders get de-banked;
VCs are contractually barred by "vice clauses"; OpenAI/Anthropic won't power
explicit generation. So well-capitalized, reputation-sensitive teams *structurally
can't* enter — leaving the field to operators willing to do high-risk payments,
web-first distribution, and human-in-the-loop (not autonomous) automation.

**Why the gaps still don't fit the constraints:** nearly all are
**subscription/SaaS** (the category has essentially *no* one-time-purchase
model), and the biggest white spaces (chargeback protection, de-banking, payout
escrow) require *being a financial entity* (capital + regulatory). The hard
anti-piracy problems (Telegram, RU/CN hosts, deepfakes) are unsolved because
they're *structurally hard*, not because incumbents are lazy — a solo dev won't
out-solve them, and funded challengers already race on the buildable axes (flat
pricing, source-removal, AI detection).

Note: the OnlyFans chatter-impersonation class action was **largely dismissed
(Dec 2025)** on ToS-disclaimer grounds — so that legal cloud is weaker than
often reported, though reputational risk and future AI-disclosure litigation
remain.

**Verdict:** Real money and real unmet needs, but for a solo operator with these
constraints it's subscription-dominated, payment-taxed, on rented land, and its
cleanest solo build is contested. The most defensible solo build *if you relax
the no-subscription rule* is **affordable, flat-rate forensic watermarking +
leak detection**; otherwise this direction doesn't fit.

## Recommendation & next steps

Ranked against the actual constraints:
1. **One-time-product niche** (STL/parametric designs, niche datasets, a
   sell-once tool) — the only option that fits sell-once / no-subscription /
   no-support, with build-skill as the moat.
2. **Romance**, sapphic or a specific monster sub-niche — real money, but a
   multi-year writing career, not passive.
3. **Adult tools**, forensic-watermark-and-leak-removal — only if willing to run
   a subscription/service with the adult payment tax.

Open decision (to pressure-test next, the way the funding carry was tested):
- (a) Scope the **flat-rate forensic-watermarking** tool (market, the hard
  parts, who pays, can a solo dev ship it).
- (b) **Romance sub-niche** deep-dive (sapphic or monster: keyword/title-count/
  competition data to find a low-competition pocket).
- (c) A **one-time-product niche** (STL/dataset/tool) against real demand and
  competition.
